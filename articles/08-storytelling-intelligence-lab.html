This one explains what I do.

Companies are now built on belief before revenue. Research labs go public before they look like companies. Seed and Series A are blurring into one long conversation with capital. Every hire is a bet on a story. In that world, storytelling is not marketing. It is infrastructure.

Every frontier company has three audiences, and each needs its own story from day zero.

  • Customers need to believe the product will work before it is finished.
  • Investors need to believe the research will compound before it has.
  • Talent needs to believe this is the room where the frontier is happening.

The common mistake is one story pointed at everyone. It is usually the investor story dressed up for customers. The form matters as much as the words. Some audiences need a private memo that only twelve people will ever read. Some need the founder on camera. Some need an essay that is still working for you two years later.

A Storytelling Intelligence Lab makes a company believed, using narrative and models together.

Storytelling used to be a craft practised by people. It is now also a capability inside models. The lab holds both. It writes, and it builds the systems that write, judge and learn. It works along three paths.

  1. Creative. A writing room that engineers the company's narratives and pushes them out, directly or through PR. It produces the essays, the films, the investor memos and the story that onboards every new hire, and it keeps all of them consistent with each other.
  2. Engineering. The systems behind the product's voice: skills that carry the company's story into every agent, evals that catch it drifting, taste ledgers that keep the specifics from being averaged away, and rewards that measure structure, not just fluency. Most of this lives in the harness around the model; some of it in post-training.
  3. Research. The test kitchen. Experiments with trends, datasets, new models, taste ledgers and RL systems, so the narrative design keeps improving and the company builds story intelligence about its customers, its investors and its talent: what each group retells, remembers and repeats.

Read across a row and you get everything one audience needs. Read down a column and you get everything one path produces. The highlighted cell is where almost nobody has put a storyteller yet, and where the leverage is.

This is Storytelling-Led Growth.

You know it is working when customers arrive already believing, when investors open the meeting with the story understood, and when candidates quote your essays back to you. The metric is not views. It is what gets retold, who shows up, and revenue per employee as the right people are pulled in.
